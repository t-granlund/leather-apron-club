What that looks like

  • A drone outfit's lead pipeline — the brief was a spoken voice note; the brand was studied before a line was written; tested like a customer, then verified live: form → routing → dashboard.
  • A barbershop's shopfront — the shop owns it, the club maintains it. Built where the club meets: Bentonville Barber Company, 113 W Central.
  • The method behind it — honest requirements, the machine doing the heavy lifting, humans gating what matters — taught inside one of the largest companies on earth, from ten people to seventy-five thousand.

What it is, and isn't

  • Is: your tools, owned by you; built in the open with receipts; a standing council that answers for the work in person.
  • Isn't: a subscription, a retainer, a pitch, or a black box. The machine is free (Apache-2.0); it runs on your accounts and keys.
  • Prices struck: complexity (bring what you know), cost (own, don't rent), indifference (a name at a table of twelve, not a ticket number).

Bring your friction to the table

One task you would love to never do again — say it plainly. The council hears it, the machine builds it, you leave with the work done and the trick of it learned. First-time guests need nothing but the task and an evening.
