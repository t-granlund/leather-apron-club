The Shop, and How It Came to Be

Victor Rubio, with his family behind him, opened Bentonville Barber Company after leaving a shop whose owner didn't keep the morals the trade deserves. Drew Garrison and Daniel Devault came with him — the originals. Ernie Herrera, John Hill, and Jonny Williams fill out the chairs: good dudes, every one.

That is how the best shops are founded — not on a business plan, but on a line a tradesman will not cross, and the people who cross the street to stand with him.

The Spot

The old room sat in a plaza by Daylight Donuts, across from the Sam's Club home office (2308 SE 28th St). The landlord raised the rent. And then the thing this whole club is built on happened: a customer had a spot open — 113 W Central Ave, Suite 201, on the square in downtown Bentonville — and handed it over.

Helping each other. Not a slogan. A floor plan.

The Patron

From the club's ledger: Tyler Granlund has been in Drew's chair since 2020 — first at Uptown Barber Company's downtown Bentonville spot, then following the shop to Centerton, where Drew Garrison worked. He loved their ethos enough to film a onewheel edit of the ride from his house to the shop; it lives in the Centerton shop's Instagram archives. Drew has been his dude since he moved here. When Victor opened the new shop, Drew came, and Tyler followed.

What the Grove Does Here

the club convenes where the thesis already worked

  1. The chairs take the trade. Bookings run on Square; walk-ins at the door. The shop runs on craft, not on a website it has to babysit.
  2. The help already crossed the counter. A barber passed a business owner an IT contact — recorded in the club's receipts, before the club had a name.
  3. The first council convenes in the chairs. Friday, September 18, 2026 — in the spot a neighbor gave, to show the room what the machine can do: speak, and it builds.
  4. The shopfront ships with the shop. Mock-up in hand — they own it, the club maintains. The next surface for the square is theirs to approve.

Why It Holds

A shop born of a landlord's greed and a neighbor's kindness now hosts a circle founded on the same math. Every party's perspective was in the room before any build: the owners' pride and their books, the customers' thumbs, the town's habits. Convey them all and the smallest thing holds like a well-set chair.
