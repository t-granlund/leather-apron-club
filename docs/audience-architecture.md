## 1. The Thesis — the cooperative turn

The enterprise world runs on a bargain: someone else builds the tools of your
work, you rent them forever, and the price is paid three ways — **complexity**
(the tools require experts to operate), **cost** (the rent never stops), and
**indifference** (you are a seat count, not a neighbor).

That bargain held because building was expensive. **That advantage is gone.**

A small crew with agentic tools now builds in a weekend what used to need a
procurement cycle. The Leather Apron Club exists to teach that, prove it in
public, and bind the people who do it into a council.

The posture is stated plainly because it is easy to get wrong:

- **Not enemies. No raised fist.** We critique *structures* — complexity, cost,
  indifference — never companies as villains. No corporate names, ever.
- **Competition, not conflict.** Companies now have to compete against
  cooperatives: groups of people who hold their own keys. That is a market
  fact, not a fight.
- **We compete on care.** The enterprise cannot care about you; you are a line
  item. The cooperative cannot *help* caring about you; you are at the table.
- **The Junto precedent.** Franklin's circle didn't petition anyone for a
  library. It built the Library Company (1731) — the first subscription
  library in America. Then a fire company (1736). Then the American
  Philosophical Society (1743). The leather-apron response to an unmet need
  was always the same: *build the institution, in public, together.*

**Internal mechanisms, codified:**
1. **The council** — every Friday evening, membership twelve, tight. Quorum is
   the build log: you show work or you send word.
2. **The standing questions** — Franklin's, adapted (see the landing page);
   always ends on *What did you build this week?*
3. **The pipeline** — inquiry → design → public → self-sustaining, inside
   30–90 days. Talk is not output.
4. **The rotation** — each table of twelve keeps at least one seat open. The
   club grows the way the Junto grew: members carry it outward.

### Tier 2 — THE MAKERS (outside creators)

**Who:** artists, writers, musicians, woodworkers, drone pilots — people whose
work is the point and who never called it engineering.

**What they need:** proof the tools serve the *craft*, onboarding that never
asks them to learn a new language first, and a place to show the work.

**Assets:** `the-method.html` (the transcendence arc is their story),
`works/` (craft-to-craft proof), the field guide (sprucegrove.io).

**Gaps (post-launch):** craft-path starter kits (one page per trade: "first
evening for the workshop / the studio / the flight bag"). Deliberately NOT
built yet — launch first, learn from the room, then build paths that fit real
people.

### Tier 3 — THE COOPERATIVES (other businesses: the outward turn)

**Who:** small groups who decide to own their tools — the shop that runs its
own booking, the co-op that runs its own membership, the crew that publishes
its own work. Businesses that want to *make, create, and expose humanity for
the beautiful thing it is.*

**What they need:** the thesis (it gives them permission), the method (it
gives them a way), and receipts (it gives them nerve).

**Assets:**
- **`cooperative.html` (new this build)** — the public thesis page:
  the old bargain, the turn, compete on care, the three removals, the lineage,
  the door.
- Deck ACT IV.9 — the same turn, three slides, for any room it needs to land in.
- `works/` — the pipeline's proof: a shop and a drone outfit, built in public.

**Gaps (post-launch):** a third case study (a cooperative, not a client),
starter kits for the boring-but-vital tools (booking, books, member records).

## 3. Insertion mechanics — how it grows

The Junto model, kept and modernized:

1. **One open seat per table.** Members bring the curious; the curious become
   members by showing up twice and bringing something made.
2. **Tables spawn tables.** When a table is full, it seeds the next one — same
   standing questions, same pipeline, same creed. The club federates like the
   Junto's circles did.
3. **The pipeline is the funnel.** Every 30–90 day build (a shop's site, a
   drone outfit's gallery) is public proof working on a real neighbor. The
   subject of every build becomes the next invitation.
4. **The works do the selling.** No ads, no pitch. `works/` + the field guide
   + a Friday room.

## 5. Voice and boundaries (binding)

- **No corporate enemies.** Name structures, never companies. The tone is the
  confident competitor, not the aggrieved outsider.
- **Confidentiality posture unchanged.** Walmart is never named. Names appear
  only as Tyler has instructed (Ashley, John Choi, Dustin Boyd). Provenance
  recorded. Tyler may pull any of it back with one commit.
- **No invented facts.** Junto history cited (Library Company 1731, Union Fire
  Company 1736, American Philosophical Society 1743 — Franklin's
  Autobiography and the standard record). Club facts only from shipped
  artifacts. Anything else: NOT VERIFIED.
- **GRAN design language everywhere.** Never-flat-black, grain veil, four type
  voices, three symbols only, the stamp, the rune-line. No emoji, anywhere.
- **One cord message across all surfaces.** The landing, the thesis page, the
  deck, the method — same creed, same questions, same door.
